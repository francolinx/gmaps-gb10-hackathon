2. Confirmed product definition
Name: GMAPS — Ground Movement Awareness and Prediction System. [U1]


Product sentence: GMAPS connects captured radio instructions, a reviewed airport map, and available movement information to surface possible future path conflicts and unresolved communication checks, with evidence and uncertainty visible to the person responsible. [U1, P1]


Plain-English explanation: “A plane and a fire truck may be heading toward the same part of a runway. GMAPS connects what they were told with what we can observe, looks ahead under explicit assumptions, and shows the controller why a situation deserves checking.”


First user: controller/instructor evaluating a supervised, fictional exercise. This is a prototype user choice, not operational approval. Pilot-facing delivery remains a later integration question; do not make a cockpit uplink a dependency tomorrow.


Intended impact to evaluate: earlier useful recognition, less effort reconstructing instructions, and fewer unsupported conclusions. No reduction in accidents, workload, response time, or false alerts has been measured for GMAPS.


What the name does not establish: certification, deployment, trademark availability, superiority to existing systems, or an approved operational safety role.

6. Three distinct outputs
Output
	Minimum evidence
	Allowed conclusion
	Route/intent relationship
	Captured, interpretable instructions plus map references.
	Routes or authorizations concern the same relevant area; timing may be unknown.
	Predicted space-time overlap
	Usable motion observations, actor identity association, geometry/footprints, time basis, route hypotheses, and a declared motion model.
	Under these assumptions, possible future occupancy overlaps.
	Observed instruction discrepancy
	Independent observation plus a source-supported instruction.
	The received observation differs from the recorded instruction under a defined comparison.
	

These are not interchangeable. A matching readback is not physical compliance. A completion report is a report, not independent observation. Missing captured completion does not establish that the truck is still on the runway. An absence of detected overlap does not certify safety.


Maintain separate records for instruction/authorization, acknowledgment, reported state, independently observed state, forecast state, and reviewer judgment. Use “recorded authorization,” not wording that implies GMAPS grants authority.
7. Predictive design — the part we must not lose
7.1 Separate truth, observations, and predictions
Simulation truth may drive evaluation but is never readable by the inference/predictor path. Observation packets are emitted with sample time, receipt time, noise/uncertainty metadata, source identity, and an explicit simulation label. The runtime sees only packets received by the current clock. Predictions are computed from that available history and the declared model.


A map animation rendered from the complete fixture trajectory is not a prediction. Likewise, an estimated truck position generated from the instruction alone is not a sensor observation. [P1]
7.2 The numerical task
For each actor i, candidate route, and relevant zone Z, calculate a projected occupancy interval or set of intervals I(i,Z) over a bounded forecast horizon. A candidate exists when actor footprints/projected corridors share the relevant spatial or protected region AND the projected intervals overlap. An interval intersection is a candidate, not an accident probability or legal-separation verdict.


For two closed illustrative intervals [a,b] and [c,d], intersection exists when max(a,c) <= min(b,d). Actual endpoint conventions, sampling resolution, buffers, footprints and geometry must be explicit. Avoid a naive plot-line crossing that misses time, height, vehicle length, or swept volume.


The first scene can use an aircraft's ground-roll segment approaching a crossing and a truck approaching from a taxiway. That preserves the predictive feature while avoiding an unreviewed landing-dynamics model. If an airborne approach is included, vertical state, runway intersection geometry, and a declared landing/rollout hypothesis are additional prerequisites. Do not flatten altitude out of a collision claim. These are scope proposals, not operational engineering standards.


Prefer a simple, auditable constant-speed/limited-acceleration model on reviewed route segments to a model that invents trajectories. If motion slows, stops, or deviates, recompute from new observations. Use swept footprints or an analytically checked zone-crossing calculation to avoid missing an encounter between discrete display frames.
7.3 Uncertainty and amendments
Represent measurement uncertainty, clock uncertainty, motion bounds and alternate route hypotheses separately. Do not label chosen ranges “95% confidence” without calibration. An unknown initial position can leave route relevance assessable but timed prediction unavailable.


A stop/cancel instruction changes communicated intent. It does not instantly stop an object. Retain a branch consistent with current observed movement until new observations support stopping. Conversely, do not force every moving target to follow its authorized route: deviations are part of the problem.


With stale tracking, widen bounds only within a declared valid model, then mark timed prediction unavailable. Do not continue extrapolation indefinitely with a fresh-looking marker. If identity association is uncertain, preserve candidate mappings rather than attach a target to the last callsign heard.
7.4 Example used only to explain the UI
Suppose a simulated model projects aircraft occupancy of one shared zone at +18 to +24 seconds and truck occupancy at +15 to +22 seconds. The ranges overlap from +18 to +22. Those numbers are invented illustrations, not LGA timing, a required prediction horizon, measured latency, or a calibrated error interval.


Suggested cue: Projected crossing-zone overlap under current assumptions. Inspect instruction, motion sources and timing.

14. A fair evaluation plan
Primary technical test: input-to-evidence-to-prediction on a small unfamiliar fictional episode, not a rehearsed animation. Reviewer labels must be prepared independently and unavailable to the runtime. Prepared parameters for expected collision/absence may live only in the evaluation runner, never in the predictor input schema.


Separate three questions:


1. Recognition: did ASR preserve the critical words and usable source intervals? Run reference text and ASR text through identical downstream code.
2. Prediction: compare a competent motion-only predictor, communication/route-only relevance, and combined intent+motion on the same episode set. Do not claim the combined system is better merely because only it receives useful information.
3. Human benefit: compare actual baseline materials with those same materials plus GMAPS. Measure checking effort and correct conclusions, not just subjective confidence. This study is not yet run.


Suggested metrics (all currently UNMEASURED): critical field/callsign-association errors; abstention and answer coverage; timestamp/source-link error; missed and false candidate events per defined exposure; repeated cues grouped per event; first eligible and first persistent cue time; forecast interval calibration/error against simulator truth; unsupported state transitions; stale-input behavior; p50/p95 input-end-to-checkable-result latency; queue growth and dropped inputs; reviewer time including correction; memory and energy if actually instrumented.


Earlier-warning claims require a preregistered alert rule, same-input baseline, and actual first supporting input time. Compare similar nuisance/recall operating points and retain misses. Subtracting edited video caption times is not a lead-time measurement. Model benchmark WER is not our workflow's failure probability.


ATC adaptation comparison: different model architectures/sizes do not isolate the effect of fine-tuning. A controlled general-versus-tuned claim needs the same base model, fresh held-out material, identical preprocessing/decoding conditions where applicable, and documented training overlap. Otherwise label it a candidate-model comparison.
