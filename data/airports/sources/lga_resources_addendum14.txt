	ASDE for airports
	ASDE-X fuses "surface surveillance radar located on top of the air traffic control tower," multilateration sensors, Mode S terminal radar, ADS-B and flight-plan data into one display with "visual and aural alarms that will alert controllers of possible runway incursions." It was "developed to help reduce critical Category A and B runway incursions." 35 airports; LaGuardia is on the list (and the diagram says "ASDE-X in use").
	The competitor slide, in one line: LGA had ASDE-X. The truck had no transponder, so ASDE-X had nothing to alarm on; the radio had the clearance. Position-based nets see motion; this one hears authorization.

	7110.65BB phraseology; chapter 3 section 7 link
	The current order is JO 7110.65BB (the FAA page header). 3-7-2: "The taxi clearance/route must include the specific route to follow." Phraseology: "RUNWAY (number), TAXI VIA (route as necessary)"; "HOLD SHORT OF (location)"; "CROSS (runway) at (runway/taxiway)"; "HOLD POSITION"; "TAXI/CONTINUE TAXIING/PROCEED VIA (route)". Readback: "Request a read back of runway hold short instructions when it is not received from the pilot/vehicle operator" — "READ BACK HOLD INSTRUCTIONS." Crossings: "An aircraft must have crossed a previous runway before another runway crossing clearance may be issued" (exception: centrelines ≤ 1,300 ft apart); "Issue a crossing clearance to vehicles for each runway their route crosses."
	This is the grammar. Every pattern in phraseology_grammar.json is one of these strings. United 2384's real clearance that night — "Runway 13, taxi via November, Alpha, Echo, hold short runway 4" — is the 3-7-2 form exactly.
	11
	"Look at the way taxi instructions are formatted, so how AI can parse that"
	Runway first, then "taxi via", then the route, then the hold-short, then (separately) crossings one runway at a time. Departures: "RUNWAY (number), LINE UP AND WAIT" (3-9-4) and "RUNWAY (number), CLEARED FOR TAKEOFF" (3-9-10), "CANCEL TAKEOFF CLEARANCE". Arrivals: "RUNWAY (number) CLEARED TO LAND" with "traffic holding in position" when LUAW traffic exists (3-10-5).
	Runway-first ordering is the parser's anchor: the word RUNWAY followed by a number starts a clearance; what follows classifies it. The public captions broke exactly where the grammar is strongest (see Addendum 13 §4), which is the ASR slide.

	15
	Runway incursion categories
	FAA: a runway incursion is "any occurrence at an airport involving the incorrect presence of an aircraft, vehicle or person on the protected area of a surface designated for the landing and take off of aircraft." A: "a collision was narrowly avoided." B: "significant potential for collision… time critical corrective/evasive response." C: "ample time and/or distance to avoid a collision." D: "no immediate safety consequences." Types: Operational Incident (controller: less than required separation, or clearing to take off/land on a closed runway), Pilot Deviation, Vehicle/Pedestrian Deviation.
	In compat_matrix.json for the UI. The monitor's rules target the Operational-Incident type — the authorization conflict — which is the one type a position-based net sees last, because nothing has moved yet when it happens. Do not assign a category to LaGuardia or Logan yourself.
	2. The one more thing the notes led to: Runway Status Lights
The LGA diagram says "Runway Status Lights in operation," and the FAA lists LaGuardia among the twenty RWSL sites. RWSL are "embedded in the pavement of runways and taxiways, designed to automatically signal pilots and vehicle operators when it is unsafe to enter, cross, or begin takeoff." Runway Entrance Lights illuminate "when there is high speed traffic on the runway or there is an aircraft on final approach"; Takeoff Hold Lights when "there is another aircraft or vehicle on the runway or about to enter the runway." They run off ASDE-X, are "a fully automatic, advisory system," and "do not substitute for an Air Traffic Control clearance." Ground vehicle operators are expected to hold when they are red.
So LaGuardia had both of the FAA's surface safety nets. Whether the entrance lights at Delta were lit at 10:30 that night, and whether the truck could see them, is the NTSB's question, not ours — do not speculate on stage. The honest line is narrower and stronger: both nets key on surveillance; the vehicle was not on surveillance; the clearance was on the radio; the engine keys on the clearance.
3. What the two new files are
phraseology_grammar.json — 16 patterns, each quoting its 7110.65 paragraph, with regexes over normalised text, the slots they fill, and "as heard" examples from the LaGuardia and Logan records; callsign patterns including the abbreviated "234" the LGA controller used twice and a look-alike guard; the number-word and phonetic tables; and the nine caption errors with their repairs. Rule of the file: never fill a slot that was not heard; null is a value, and null hold-short means acknowledged = UNKNOWN.
compat_matrix.json — six reservation types on a runway (LANDING, TAKEOFF, LUAW, CROSSING, OCCUPYING, CLOSED), four severities (CONFLICT, CAUTION, ADVISED, REMINDER), and thirteen rules R1–R13, each with its 7110.65 citation and the scenario that exercises it. The three that carry the demo: R1 (crossing vs active landing → LaGuardia at +40 s), R4 (takeoff vs landing on intersecting runways; ADVISED when traffic information was issued → the LaGuardia 0:13 negative case; CONFLICT when it was not → Logan at 11:33:43), R7 (hold-short not read back → the clipped case). Timers R8 (close out the crossing) and R9 (LUAW 90 s) are the two Joe named. The warning object names both authorizations, both source transmissions, the rule, and what would clear it.
Both files are derived, not authoritative: a controller will find edge cases (LAHSO, multiple runway crossings within 1,300 ft, helicopter ops). Say "derived from JO 7110.65BB chapter 3" on the slide, and nothing stronger.
