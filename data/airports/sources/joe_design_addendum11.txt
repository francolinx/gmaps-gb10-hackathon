Bottom line. Two practitioners who have never spoken to each other — an aviation speech-AI researcher this morning and an air-traffic CTI program manager at noon — arrived at the same tool from opposite ends: a system that listens to tower and ground communications, turns each clearance into authorized state, and raises its hand when two authorizations need the same runway at the same time. Joe went further and gave it an architecture in writing. Scored under the same rubric as every idea since Sept 30, the merged build comes out at 79.4 — two points under TowerDebrief (81.4), above everything else, and it is the same engine as TowerDebrief with a runway rule, an airport graph and an incursion story on top. So "this is it" is consistent with the scoreboard, on two conditions: the claims stay at replay, training and advisory (never "it would have prevented LaGuardia"), and the AI is a parser, not a judge. The rest of this addendum is what changes in Saturday's plan.

	4. The architecture he put in writing (his model's note, 14:04), and the verdict on each point
1. AI translates language into structured state changes; a deterministic graph/state engine does the safety reasoning. Adopt. This was Addendum 9's complaint about every project (A1, A3); it is now a design, not a complaint.
2. A clearance is a set of permissions and constraints, not a sentence. "Taxi via A, B, hold short runway 15" = authority over A → B → the hold boundary and nothing beyond; "cross runway 15 at B" adds a temporary runway authority; a takeoff clearance adds another kind. Adopt verbatim.
3. Keep authorized, acknowledged, reported, inferred and observed state separate. "A voice-only system should never quietly turn 'cleared onto the runway' into 'physically on the runway.'" This is the single most important safety property and it is exactly the Clearance Reviewer's lane discipline. Adopt the names.
4. Runway conflicts are overlapping reservations with a compatibility matrix and time windows, not a Boolean. Adopt; LUAW with a landing clearance is the worked example in the FAA rules.
5. The expected sequence is a state machine with legal transitions, not a script. Adopt as an event-sourced state machine; the Petri-net remark is for the paper, not Saturday.
6. The language model is a constrained parser with a fixed ontology and a confidence; it may never say "this is safe." Adopt; this is the line to put on the slide.
7. Every warning is explainable: the two authorizations, their timestamps, the resource, the rule, the two source transmissions. Adopt; this is the packet the Clearance Reviewer already exports.
8. "Semantic surveillance" and the three modes — full (surveillance + ADS-B + comms), degraded, continuity (comms only) — with the same engine running in all three. Adopt the framing; it is how DeadAir survives: as the continuity mode of the monitor, not as a product.
9. Validation corpus: the FAA ASIAS runway-incursion database (searchable event narratives), used to write simulator scenarios and ask "at what point in the sequence did the system have enough information?" Adopt as the post-hackathon test, and as one sentence in the pitch.
10. Pick one airport, model only its movement area, six rule families — runway-crossing, takeoff/crossing, LUAW/arrival, hold-short boundary, route incompatibility, unresolved or discrepant readback — then "start abusing it": blocked transmissions, similar callsigns, mid-route amendments, wrong-runway readbacks, vehicles without a clean identifier. Adopt; this is the Saturday scope and the Saturday test list.
What it adds to the record: the five state kinds, the reservation model and the "parser, not judge" rule are the three things Addendum 9 said were missing from every project. They arrived from a controller-educator's side, in writing, the day before the build.

8. Cautions that this meeting added
* Never say "it would have prevented LaGuardia." The NTSB has not said who was working ground, which frequency carried the crossing, or why the stop calls did not land. Say: "at this point in the recorded sequence the monitor had two authorizations on runway 4."
* Do not use the VASAviation video's audio in the build or the video; it is their edit of LiveATC audio. Use it to check the sequence, then re-voice.
* "Frequency," not "channel." "Clearance-state monitor" or "surface safety assurance layer," not "safety management system."
* FIS-B / UAT 978 to the cockpit is one labelled-future slide, not a claim; Joe said it himself: "this might be getting a little bit too complicated for your presentation."
* Latency: Joe's "couple of seconds might be too much" is about flight; on the ground he gave leeway. Show the number rather than arguing it.
* The three-model stack Franco described (recogniser plus three to five models under 30 B) is more than the design needs. One recogniser, one small parser model, one deterministic engine. A cross-check model only if it costs nothing.
